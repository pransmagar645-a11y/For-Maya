break the seal

My dearest Maya,

I take up quill and parchment tonight because the miles betwixt Hong Kong and Glasgow are wide, and a letter is the one thing I can send that needs no signal to arrive.

Three months. 'Tis a small siege, as sieges go, and we have trained for harder things than distance. Thy knee gave thee ample reason to stop training altogether, and still thou returned to the mats again and again, for the love of the art runs deeper in thee than any injury could reach. Few would call that wise. It is, plainly, thee.

Let it be known, sealed and witnessed: I shall not touch a drop of wine nor ale till thy feet touch ground on the seventeenth of December, if thou wilt hold likewise to no smoke between now and then. A pinky promise, sworn in wax, small vow, kept fully, same as any good structure: it is not the size of the beam that matters, but that it bears the load it was sworn to bear. And I look forward to being back on the mats with thee, doing our bit, "two two's my word crodie," as we have always done.

I confess I think often of the day thou dost land, of finding some corner of a night market or a proper trattoria and ordering too much: food bordering on gluttony, ice cream beyond sense. We shall keep our promise of trying proper Italian fare and Nepali fare besides, and, because we agreed and I intend to hold thee to it, pistachio and taro for dessert, both.

Until then: train hard, mind thy base, keep thy structure sound in all things, on the mats and off.

Thine own, Peans
